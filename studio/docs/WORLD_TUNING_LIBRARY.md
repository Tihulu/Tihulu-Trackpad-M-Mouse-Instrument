# World tuning library / Instrument and Helper roles

## User-facing architecture (design contract, not activation)
- Input identity, instrument/play surface, role, musical mapping, and output are independent. Devices selects hardware and routing; Instruments contains reusable configurations; Play chooses the active surface and role. Every capable source may be Instrument (notes), Helper (CC, pedal, bend, expression, XY), or Hybrid (explicit non-conflicting assignments). Show unsupported combinations with reasons. Do not equate device name with role.
- Trackpad and Magic Mouse should support standard, scale-locked and microtonal fretted/fretless playing through a verified local MPE receiver, including Logic Pro. Existing touch and local MPE routes remain; arbitrary degree support needs a new step-identity voice plan and safe pitch-bend headroom (base tuning + expression), scoped ownership, neutral rearm, receiver bend range confirmation and physical frequency/hold tests. Hybrid must prevent double-use of gesture/contact and channel-wide bend conflicts. No remote dynamic MPE bypass.
- A single reusable scale/tuning library should feed physical MIDI Scale Assistant, Trackpad, Magic Mouse, virtual/mobile piano, arpeggiators and compatible instruments; percussive and non-pitched helpers opt out. Saved tuning selection is distinct from current frozen active route. No mutation while notes held. No silent 12-TET fallback.

## Implemented first reference catalog
WorldTuningLibrary.swift contains 74 records. Each has a stable ID, name, region, kind, numeric cents, source and contextual caveat. Distinguish theoretical, idealized approximation, research prototype, documented sample and mathematical models. Only explicitly octave-periodic records can construct the existing offline MicrotonalKeyboardTuning. Finite fragments and measured multi-octave ranges cannot be silently repeated or normalized. No claim of authentic seyir, radif, maqam or ensemble performance. No runtime audio/MIDI activation. The macOS disclosure is a read-only temporary discovery location; move to canonical reusable library when navigation is consolidated (UX-009/025/026).

## Priority collection research and expansion
1. Turkish / Ottoman: AEU theoretical 53-comma approximations, then performance-informed alternatives (Bozkurt et al.), and historical Safiyyüddin Urmevî 17-tone, Rauf Yekta, Yarman-36, Töre-Karadeniz and other named systems. Compare editions, spellings, octave closure and actual measured pitch distributions. TDV makam entries distinguish seyir, karar, güçlü and variable degrees. Additional research queue: Hicaz variants (Uzzal, Hümâyun, Zirgüleli Hicaz), Kürdî, Karcığar, Nevâ, Tâhir, Muhayyer, Bayatî, Isfahan, Acem, Acemaşiran, Mahur, Hüzzam, Sultânîyegâh, Nikriz, Nişâbur, Ferahnâk, Eviç, Irak, Rehâvî, Bestenigâr, Dilkeşhâverân. Do not force non-octave Sabâ or conflicting source rows into octave presets. Panteli Table 2.3 Mahur and Hüzzam rows do not total 53; hold for cross-source review rather than normalize.
2. Iranian: seven dastgahs with separately identified Abdoli (2011) classification prototypes; next source-specific Shur natural/koron fifth, Abu Ata, Bayat-e Tork, Afshari, Dashti, Bayat-e Esfahan, selected gusheh and radif-specific intonations. Compare Farhat, Tala'i, During and Shafiei's measured pitch distributions. Do not label koron/sori universally fixed at 50 cents. Aliases that share a pitch list still have distinct modal identities.
3. Levant / Arabic: idealized 24-EDO examples and finite ajnas. Expand region-specific variants for Syria, Lebanon, Palestine, Jordan, Iraq, Egypt, North Africa, plus Ottoman/Levantine Jewish piyut where appropriately sourced. Research Rast, Bayati, Hijaz, Kurd, Nahawand, Ajam, Nikriz, Sikah, Huzam, Saba, Saba Zamzam, Suznak, Bayati Shuri, Husseini, Hijazkar, Shahnaz, Iraq, Bastanikar, Mustaar, Lami, Jiharkah and other named branches; use explicit ajnas, variant, source, uncertainty and modulation descriptions. Never treat common Arabic and Turkish names as interchangeable.
4. Historical Europe / Mediterranean: Pythagorean, 5-limit JI, meantone, Byzantine 72-part models in first catalog; later historically specified Werckmeister, Kirnberger, Vallotti, Young, Silbermann, Zarlino and ancient Greek diatonic/chromatic/enharmonic genera (Archytas, Aristoxenus, Ptolemy variants). Research non-octave and note spelling requirements.
5. South, East and Southeast Asia: Indian Hindustani/Carnatic raga reference variants and shruti (not 22 equal notes), Japanese gagaku shō and koto measured tunings, Chinese sanfen sunyi / twelve lü, Korean court music, Indonesian individual slendro/pelog ensembles, Thai ranat (seven-equal idealization distinct from measured instruments), Vietnamese and Cambodian collections. Do not flatten ensembles or performance traditions to one fixed country scale.
6. Americas, Africa, Oceania: DaMuSc measured samples by instrument, collection ID, performer/source and original reference. Bolivia siku/panpipes, Peruvian K'ero and panpipes, Brazilian pífano, Angola likembe, Uganda amadinda and harps, West African balafon, Ethiopian qenet, Pacific panpipes. Preserve raw finite span and do not infer tonic or period without evidence. More collections must be license/provenance checked before distribution.
7. Modern mathematical systems: 5–128 EDO presets, exact ratios, 7/11/13-limit JI, Partch 43-tone, Bohlen-Pierce (tritave), Wendy Carlos Alpha/Beta/Gamma, Scala SCL/KBM. Non-octave periods require a new domain model; current octave preview must reject them.
